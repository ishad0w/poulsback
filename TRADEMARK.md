# Using the poulsback name and logo

Forks, ports, experiments and redistribution are welcome.

This guide helps people tell which builds come from upstream and who maintains
an independent version.

The **poulsback** name, wordmark and cube logo identify the upstream project.

## Code, artwork and identity

The [licence](LICENSE) and [notices](NOTICES.md) govern copying, modification
and redistribution of the corresponding material.

The [logo assets](assets/logo/README.md) are already covered by the root MIT
licence; that copyright permission remains in place.

This policy addresses representation of origin and endorsement.

It does not change those copyright grants, add conditions to the MIT licence, or
limit uses permitted by applicable law.

Licence notices and [author credit](NOTICES.md#authorship-and-redistribution)
remain separate concerns.

## References and distribution packages

You may refer truthfully to Poulsback in documentation, articles, compatibility
statements and descriptions such as "based on poulsback" or "a fork of
poulsback".

You may show the logo to identify the upstream project in those contexts without
suggesting that the project endorses you or your product.

Unchanged upstream releases may be redistributed under the upstream name.

Distribution packages and clearly identified distribution builds may keep that
name, including builds with packaging, compatibility or security patches.

Identify the distributor, source revision and changes so users can tell which
build they received and where to report its packaging-specific problems.

You do not need individual permission for these uses when the origin is clear
and you comply with the applicable licences.

## Independent forks and products

For an independently maintained product or fork, please give it a clear identity
and describe its relationship to Poulsback.

A distinct product name and visual identity help people find the right
downloads, documentation and support.

Clearly label independent download pages, support channels and modified builds.

They should not imply an official upstream release, sponsorship or approval.

Credit your own changes while retaining the original authors' applicable
notices.

Where independent builds must coexist, use distinct package and service names
and avoid silently replacing upstream files or state.

A distribution package intended to replace another should declare that
relationship in its packaging.

These are integration recommendations; this policy does not require renaming
kernel interfaces, UAPI identifiers or every compatibility path.
